How the terminology was researched

Recover the phrase, follow the evidence, record the proposed translation, and leave the review trail visible.

1 · Find and prioritize the phrases

Start with the Archive

The recorded Foundation Notebook work used an Archive phrase inventory and first-ring terms to seed 20 scientific concepts for 10 target languages. The inventory records corpus signals such as document and occurrence counts. “Genetic drift” had 76 recorded occurrences in nine documents; these are the historical inventory’s counts, not a current Archive census.

The target languages were Spanish, French, Portuguese, German, Italian, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, and Hindi. The compendium was designed to accept additional languages without starting the work over.

2 · Gather evidence

Compare sources, with their roles clear

  • Wikipedia and Wikidata: align concepts across language editions and identify candidate terms. For genetic drift the record linked the English article and the French article; its Wikidata item is Q486420. A title match is a signal, not proof of a preferred term in every context.
  • Specialist glossaries and dictionaries: the recorded Spanish evidence for genetic drift was the Instituto Roche glossary. Its current entry pairs “genetic drift” with deriva genética. The earlier stored link has since changed.
  • University sources: course materials and repositories supply examples of usage in context. For common descent, the original review recorded a University of La Plata article as a signal to investigate, not proof that the candidate translation was correct.
  • Booksellers, publishers, and libraries: the recorded separate publication-title memory used catalog records to find translated editions. The Casa del Libro listing supports El pulgar del panda for Gould’s The Panda’s Thumb. The record also lists Library of Congress evidence for Darwin’s On the Origin of Species; its Spanish talking-book listing identifies El origen de las especies as translated from English. Catalog evidence is useful for titles; an edition listing alone does not establish every technical term in that book.

3 · Decide and preserve uncertainty

A positive result and a question left open

Recorded approval: Spanish deriva genética and French dérive génétique were marked approved with glossary and terminology links. German Gendrift replaced the seeded genetische Drift, retained as an alternate. These are statuses recorded in the 2026 workbench, not evidence that a named independent language expert reviewed them.

Still provisional: “common descent” → descendencia común. The corresponding Spanish Wikipedia title, Antepasado común, raised a concept-alignment question; the review log kept the translation under review. A different title can describe a related concept, so the mismatch calls for checking meaning, not an automatic replacement.

Workbench: a bounded May 2026 snapshot combined the compendium and review queue: four active languages, 12 queued items, and 44 deferred. Those figures are historical and do not describe the current queue.

4 · A separate operational glossary

Follow the handoff carefully

The later TalkOrigins translation runner contains a separate 27-term, four-language compendium. The retained records do not prove that it was generated from this 20-term research compendium. For example, the research record prefers German Gendrift and lists genetische Drift as an alternate; the runner compendium specifies genetische Drift. That difference is a visible comparison for review, not proof of an error or an automatic change to production terminology.
