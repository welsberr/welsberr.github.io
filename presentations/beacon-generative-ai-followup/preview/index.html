How does this illustrate Monte Carlo simulation?

Monte Carlo methods use repeated random samples or simulations to explore a model's range of outcomes and estimate quantities of interest. Here, random sampling can produce different final allele frequencies even when the settings are unchanged.

Each run adds five simulated populations per treatment. The dots show the cumulative treatment means and their difference as results accumulate. More runs can make these summaries less sensitive to individual outcomes, but the dots need not move steadily or settle in a small sample.

The two treatments use the same seed for each run. The plot describes this model and its assumptions; it is not a significance test or a measurement of a real population.

Interpret the comparison

Compare the spread within and between treatments. What varies across runs, and what additional settings might change the pattern? A simulation alone cannot establish which force caused a change in a real population.
