01 · Adoption

What are we delegating?

Generative AI produces text, images, code, and other outputs. An agentic workflow connects that generation to tools, memory, and actions.

Drafting an email, finding a source, and changing an account cross different boundaries of authority.

Evaluate the whole workflow: what it can do, what it knows, and what happens when it is wrong.

A conceptual progression

  1. GeneratePropose an answer
  2. RetrieveBring evidence into context
  3. ActChange something outside the conversation

More authority calls for stronger checks at the boundary.

Speaker notes

Ask the room for one task they already delegate. Distinguish a model from the application wrapped around it. This progression is a teaching model, not a historical law.

02 · A shared framework for adoption

Start with the work we want to improve.

Before looking at examples, I want to set out the questions I bring to any AI-assisted workflow, including my own.

Define the tasks, agree on what better work looks like, and assess quality, productivity, and cost together. Distinguish expected benefits from observed results and causal evidence.

Build in task-specific evaluation, review, and a clear path to escalate problems and correct outcomes.

Shared design questions

Introduce these as shared design questions before the industry overview and company example. Apply them to the speaker’s own work as well as organizational adoption. 03 · What the measurements mean

Private-sector adoption is uneven.

U.S. Census data show 17–20% of businesses reporting AI use from December 2025 to May 2026. This covers AI broadly, across business functions.

Among firms with 250 or more employees, 37% reported using AI. Adoption varies by company size and sector.

Using AI, integrating it into workflows, and demonstrating better outcomes are separate milestones.

05 · My experience: internal adoption

The developer’s work is shifting.

I work at a company pursuing broad internal AI adoption. Management is looking for greater productivity and higher quality in software production.

Development is shifting toward a person directing an AI assistant to do the coding, then applying domain expertise to review the workflow and the product it produces.

Keeping generative-AI costs contained is part of the challenge.

07 · A bridge to research practice

Make scientific virtues operational.

ClaimWright draws explicitly on Robert Pennock’s scientific-virtues work: curiosity, honesty, attention to evidence, and willingness to revise.

The engineering question is how to turn those commitments into repeatable actions—and preserve evidence that the actions happened.

A declaration states a value. A gate checks a condition. An enforcement point can stop an action.

08 · ClaimWright

Write the agreement before the output.

ClaimWright supplies a human-readable collaboration agreement, policy files, claim states, review roles, and pre- and post-action checks.

A claim may be exploratory, supported, contested, stale, contradicted, or private-only. The workflow makes those distinctions visible before public release.

Current boundary: this is an initial policy framework. Its Rust checker verifies the presence of the policy files; it does not prove that a claim is true.

09 · Policy gating & enforcement

A gate must be able to say “stop.”

GroundRecall defines policy decision points around memory operations. Its documented outcomes include allow, require review, soft gate, hard gate, and deny; hard gates and denials block covered operations.

Keep the decision to propose an action separate from the authority to execute it. Enforcement has to happen on the actual path to the protected operation.

Try this simplified release rule. Change an input and inspect the reason.

10 · GroundRecall + Epistemap

Remember why, not just what.

GroundRecall keeps durable claims, source provenance, review status, and release scope outside an individual chat. Indexed search finds candidate records; graph relations recover context.

Epistemap supplies a shared graph representation, traversal, and diagnostics. It stays below domain policy.

A retrieved record is evidence to inspect. A graph edge or confidence estimate is not a truth certificate.

11 · doclift + Didactopus

Keep the source trail through transformation.

doclift normalizes legacy Word and WordPerfect documents into text, Markdown, and structured sidecars that preserve extraction information.

Didactopus turns educational material into draft learning domains, prerequisite graphs, practice, and reviewable skill artifacts.

Conversion is a transformation to inspect. Ingestion does not automatically make material accurate, mastered, or suitable for publication.

12 · CiteGeist

From a rough reference to a defensible citation.

CiteGeist converts free-text references into draft BibTeX, resolves identifiers and metadata, retains conflicts and provenance, and expands a bibliography through citation links and topic search.

It supports directed literature discovery as well as repair of references already in hand.

Verification has two questions: Does this work exist? and Does this work support this claim?

17 · Application: AI assurance

Name the mechanism and its failure modes.

The Mechanism-Aware AI Assurance preprint proposes an Operational Premise Taxonomy: describe the computational mechanisms a system actually uses, then examine the assumptions and failure modes that follow.

It adds a question to the governance discussion: what evidence links the stated policy to the implemented system?

This is a public, inspectable proposal. Its usefulness as an assurance method remains something to evaluate.

18 · Keep the limits visible

The controls create work, too.

A source can be wrong. A citation can match the wrong work. A graph can preserve a mistaken interpretation. A policy can be bypassed by an uncovered path.

Review takes time, and repeated model agreement can reproduce a shared error. Local processing alone does not establish privacy or correctness.

Track error rates, review effort, corrections, and boundary failures alongside the number of artifacts produced.

What to measure next

  • Citation identity accuracy
  • Claim–source fit
  • Time to find supporting evidence
  • Stale-claim detection
  • Release-boundary failures
  • Human review workload

19 · A BEACON-sized experiment

Try a bounded, falsifiable comparison.

Choose one literature question. Compare a baseline workflow with a provenance-preserving workflow on the same task and time budget.

Have reviewers evaluate citation identity, source fit, uncertainty, and time needed to audit each result. Record rejected candidates and corrections.

For teaching, add an unaided transfer task. For agents, test whether a prohibited action is actually blocked.
